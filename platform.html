The VitalCue platform

Combining everyday signals with clinical data to study recovery

We combine measurements from everyday life with clinical and laboratory data to investigate recovery patterns and hospital readmission risk.

Data with context

Wearable measurements in clinical context

VitalCue brings wearable activity and mobility data together with clinical history, laboratory results, and transplant variables to study readmission risk.

Activity

Step counts, walking distance, and activity patterns across the day.

Mobility

Walking speed, step length, and related measures when supported by the data source.

Personal patterns

Changes in routine and departures from an individual baseline.

Clinical context

Clinical history, laboratory results, and transplant variables, including the Kidney Donor Profile Index (KDPI).

Modeling framework

Wearable signals and clinical predictors

Our approach combines two sources of information: wearable measurements that track changes over time, and clinical, laboratory, and transplant variables that provide context for those changes.

01 / WEARABLE DATA

Personal baselines and changes over time

Analyze activity and mobility patterns to establish individual baselines, identify short-term deviations, and track longer-term recovery trends.

02 / CLINICAL AND TRANSPLANT DATA

Patient and donor characteristics

Incorporate clinical history, laboratory results, and transplant variables, including donor organ measures such as KDPI, alongside wearable data when studying readmission risk.

This describes a development framework. Production alerts, device integrations, and model performance are not established by this website.

Research priorities

Evaluating data quality and model performance

Data quality comes first

Missing data, device wear time, and differences between sensors need to be considered before interpreting a change in activity.

Evaluate the whole workflow

Performance, calibration, and the burden of false alerts matter alongside the practical experience of patients and care teams.

Privacy as a design priority

Patient privacy guides how we plan to collect, manage, and use research data.
