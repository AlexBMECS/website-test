We are developing a framework that connects activity data with models of individual change, so we can study recovery beyond scheduled visits.

Data with context

Start with how a person moves.

VitalCue’s early work explored smartphone measures of mobility. Our current direction brings wearable data into the study of readmission risk.

Activity

Step counts, walking distance, and activity patterns across the day.

Mobility

Walking speed, step length, and related measures when supported by the data source.

Personal patterns

Changes in routine and departures from an individual baseline.

Clinical context

Study-specific outcomes and clinical variables to evaluate the value of wearable signals.

Modeling framework

Two views of change.

Our proposed framework pairs short-term anomaly detection with longer-term behavioral modeling.

01 / SHORT-TERM CHANGE

Recognize departures from usual activity

Investigate sudden changes in observed patterns and determine whether they are informative about a patient’s recovery.

02 / LONGER-TERM PATTERNS

Understand the trajectory

Study personal baselines and trends over days and weeks, rather than interpreting each measurement in isolation.

This describes a development framework. Production alerts, device integrations, and model performance are not established by this website.

Research priorities

Useful insight needs careful validation.

Data quality comes first

Missing data, device wear time, and differences between sensors need to be considered before interpreting a change in activity.

Evaluate the whole workflow

Performance, calibration, and the burden of false alerts matter alongside the practical experience of patients and care teams.

Privacy as a design priority

Project materials explore on-device analysis and federated learning. Any implementation requires its own security review and study-specific data governance.
